Debrief Form: Online Curtains Game (Study 1)

Thank you for participating in this study. The purpose of this study is to understand what makes some games of chance feel more absorbing than others.

In each round of the curtain game, curtains were removed until only one was left, revealing your points. The two versions of the game you played differed only in how many curtains (possible outcomes) there were in each round: participants were randomly assigned to play either 2 and 32 curtains, or 4 and 16 curtains, in a random order. A round with more possible outcomes resolves more uncertainty, which we describe as delivering more information. We predicted that the version with more possible outcomes would feel more immersive and absorbing, and we will compare your ratings after each game to test this. Many modern slot machines also have a large number of possible outcomes on every spin, so the results will also help us understand why some gambling products are so engaging.

Below we explain how the curtain game worked and list some resources. Once again, we would like to thank you for participating in the study.

How the Curtain Game Worked

Every curtain was equally likely to be the last one left, and nothing you did could change which curtain remained. The point values were balanced around zero, so on average each round was worth 0 points, and every version of the game had the same average outcome and the same range of outcomes (from −64 to +64 points). Your bonus started at £1.00 and changed by 1p for every 10 points you won or lost, so any bonus above or below £1.00 came down to chance alone.

The curtains that were removed in each round were chosen by a random number generator. No one could predict which curtain would be left, and earlier rounds had no effect on later ones. Even so, many people feel they can see patterns or streaks in random outcomes, and much of our research looks at how people come to hold these beliefs in games of chance and in gambling.
